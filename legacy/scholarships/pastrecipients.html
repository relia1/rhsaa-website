Past Scholarship Recipients

The Roosevelt High School Alumni Association scholarship program began in 2022 as a way to reward the Association's youngest members. Below is information on each of the past recipients of this scholarship with bios written at the time of their award.

Class of 2026


Carolina Alonso


Carolina will be studying Political Science and Spanish at Northwestern University this fall. Her academic achievements illustrate her dedication to excellence. She earned a 4.0 GPA, participated in the Spanish immersion program, and was presented with an editorial leadership award for her work as 2025-2026 Yearbook Managing Editor.

In her scholarship application, Carolina noted that every job she’s had involved working with kids. Whether teaching reading, officiating youth sports, or serving as a camp counselor, those experiences reinforced the importance of leadership and community involvement. After discovering the joy of Slam Poetry competition, Carolina became Roosevelt’s annual champion twice. She went on to perform her poems at the district-wide competition, Verselandia. She credits Slam Poetry with helping her better understand herself. Newfound confidence enabled her to run for student council, become a varsity athlete, captain of four teams and recipient of numerous athletic awards. Carolina also was a member of the 2024-26 We The People Constitution team. At school’s end, she received the Social Studies Department Civic Engagement award.

Carolina's willingness to embrace new challenges has already led to remarkable accomplishments and will continue to serve her well in college and beyond.

Grey Hawk


Grey plans to begin studying Architecture this fall at the University of Oregon’s Clark Honors College. Grey graduated Roosevelt with a weighted GPA of 4.10. They also completed advanced placement classes, dual enrollment classes at PCC, and the ACE Mentor Program of Oregon, which encourages students considering careers in architecture, construction and engineering.

In their scholarship application, Grey wrote, “...what matters most in life [is] having a real life impact on others, especially youth.” As a downhill ski instructor, they were challenged by a very frightened, crying four-year-old girl. Grey calmed her and helped her gain enough confidence that day to make several downhill runs by herself. They saw that same potential for positive impact through architecture when collaborating with others designing a complex office space for the Kenton Women’s Village. Grey noted that the work experience and seeing completion of the project on-site showed them the positive impact of the work on others, and inspired them to pursue a degree in architecture.

Grey's passion for helping others, combined with their commitment to thoughtful design, will serve them well as they pursue their future in architecture.

Jasmin Reyes-Contreras


Jasmin will be attending Barnard College in New York City, where she plans to study Public Policy with a focus on education and a minor in Latino and Ethnic Studies. A 3.9 GPA student, Jasmin has built her high school career around advocating for students and expanding opportunities for others.

Throughout high school, Jasmin consistently used her voice to advocate for others. As Oregon’s representative to the National Student Council, she traveled to Washington, D.C. to advocate for continued Title I funding and educational equity. She also served as Roosevelt’s Student Body President and Rose Festival Princess. Drawing on her own experiences overcoming adversity, she became a leader focused on creating opportunities and support systems for other students.

Jasmin's commitment to service is perhaps best reflected in her work as co-founder of The Giving Tree, a student-led holiday assistance program that has supported more than 200 students and families across Roosevelt's feeder schools. By creating systems that will continue long after her graduation, she has left a lasting impact on her community.

Jasmin’s leadership, compassion, and dedication to public service will serve her well as she works to shape educational policy and advocate for future generations.

Kyliana Polychronis


Kyliana will be attending University of Southern California, pursuing a Communications Major, with a minor in Sports Media. A dedicated student, she maintained a 3.98 GPA while completing several AP and dual-enrollment courses.

Kyliana served as editor-in-chief for Voices of Roosevelt, helping lead a team of 28 students, publishing over 25 articles. She worked to create an inclusive environment where all students felt welcomed and supported as members of the publication. She interned with the local newspaper, St. Johns Review, publishing 3 articles. Most recently she served for a year as the Co-Communication Commissioner on the Student Council, helping manage schoolwide communications through video announcements, social media, and event promotions. She stays active with her involvement in softball, Girl Scouts, and helping support youth with disabilities - which is inspired by her relationship with her sibling. As part of Girl Scouts, she helped design and build a sustainable, trauma-informed sensory and play area for a local preschool.

Kyliana's perseverance, kindness, initiative, advocacy, and dedication will serve her well as she pursues a future in communications and sports media.

Luke T VanDeGrift


Luke will be attending the University of Portland, where he plans to pursue a Mechanical Engineering and Mathematics double major with an Aerospace Engineering minor through the Honors College. Valedictorian of the Class of 2026, he has completed several AP and university level courses in science, math, and engineering. His passion for engineering extends beyond the classroom. Luke engineered and deployed a rocket “to mach 1.8 and 18,000 feet”- roughly 1,190 mph.

Luke spent time working as an assistant coach for Portland Tennis and Education - no cost tennis training for local youth. Through this work, he helped create an inclusive and approachable environment for young athletes in the community. Luke was also a member of Roosevelt's tennis team. He earned recognition as a National Merit Commended Student, scoring in the top 3% for the PSAT, as well as a unique science award. Luke credits his parents for his love of science. His engineering projects taught him the value of experimentation, problem-solving, and learning from failure. Luke also participates in birding, playing ultimate frisbee, and rocketry extracurriculars.

Luke’s dedication, creativity, and perseverance have already led to impressive accomplishments and will continue to serve him well in his future engineering studies.

That mindset has shaped both her academic success and her strength of character.

We are inspired by Ariana’s resilience and determination, and we are honored to support her as she begins this next chapter.
